SlimRead

A minimal, open-source iOS browser that hides the status bar in every orientation — so vertical-scroll comics and webtoons fill the entire screen, with nothing drawn over the artwork.

The problem it solves

On iOS the system status bar — clock, battery, signal — is drawn on top of the page for the entire reading session. No setting removes it, in a reader app or in iOS itself. Safari, Home Screen web apps and Guided Access do not help:

ApproachOutcome
Home Screen web app (PWA)Status bar still renders. black-translucent only lets content pass underneath; the clock and battery still draw on top.
Fullscreen APINot supported on iPhone Safari outside video elements.
Guided AccessHides the home indicator, not the status bar.
Rotating to landscapeWorks, but portrait is the correct aspect ratio for vertical-scroll comics.

A native app has no such restriction. A view controller returning true from prefersStatusBarHidden hides it in every orientation. SlimRead is that one change, built from the outside: an ordinary WebKit browser that does not draw system UI over the page.

The longer version, question by question: why the status bar sits over your comic, and everything that does not fix it.

What it does

  • No status bar, portrait or landscape. Documented UIKit API, no orientation spoofing.
  • No permanent chrome. Nothing overlays the page while reading. Controls appear only when asked for.
  • Edge to edge. Artwork fills the display, corner to corner.
  • Continuous chapters. Reaching the end of one appends the next inline, so reading does not stop at a wall of comments and recommendations.
  • Progressive panel loading, tuned so the visible page is never starved by everything below it loading at once.
  • Live tweaks. Layout and reading behaviour come from CSS and JS fetched from the repository at launch, so changes ship without rebuilding or re-signing.

What it is not

SlimRead is a web browser. It is not a client for any service, not a replacement for any official app, and not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to any company.

  • It hosts and redistributes nothing. Every page and image is loaded live from the service's own servers, over your own logged-in session, exactly as a browser would.
  • It touches no payment, account or content system. Subscriptions, purchases and creator payouts happen on the service, under its terms.
  • It circumvents nothing. No paywall or DRM bypass, no ad-stripping of monetised content, no downloading or offline copying. Locked chapters stay locked.
  • It makes no money. No monetisation of any kind.

What it changes is presentation, on the reader's own device — the same category of thing a browser's reader mode, zoom, or a personal userstyle does with content already delivered to that device.

Installing

iOS 15 or later. SlimRead is not on the App Store and is installed by sideloading, which requires signing with your own Apple ID — nobody can hand you a ready-to-run copy. The repository includes a script that walks through it.

Apps signed with a free Apple ID stop opening after seven days. The README covers every way around that, including the free ones that need no computer.

Why it is public

The unauthorised copies of this work are, today, easier and more pleasant to read than the paid original — better layout, nothing covering the art, no friction. SlimRead exists because one reader would rather patch around a single unmet request than switch sides. The lesson for anyone shipping a reading product: when the paid, legal path is worse than the free, illegal one, you teach your own audience to leave.
